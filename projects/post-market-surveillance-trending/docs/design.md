# Design and review boundaries

The report is an illustrative screening record, not legal advice or a compliance claim. It assumes a manufacturer and uses date received as the date of awareness. The fields `device_related` and `eu_serious` represent facts assessed by a human, including reasonable possibility of causation. Missing facts must not be encoded as no. For FDA, 21 CFR 803.50(a) covers deaths or serious injuries where a device may have caused or contributed, and malfunctions likely to cause or contribute to death or serious injury on recurrence. The tool screens those facts with the 30-calendar-day maximum. It does not cover 5-day reports under 803.53, supplemental reports, exemptions, field actions or all manufacturer obligations. See https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-H/part-803/subpart-E/section-803.50 and https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-H/part-803/subpart-E/section-803.53.

EU MDR Article 87(1)-(5) describes serious incident reporting and the 15, 2 and 10 day maximums. The tool uses the most urgent applicable category and assumes the serious incident and reasonable causal link are already assessed. Immediate reporting, exceptions, uncertainty, field safety corrective actions, and Article 88 trend reporting require separate review. See https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj/eng. A blank deadline means the supplied facts did not meet the simplified screening condition, not that an event is definitively nonreportable.

The baseline is the pooled complaint count divided by pooled units across the first three supplied months per family. The upper control limit is baseline rate plus three times the square root of baseline rate divided by current exposure. EWMA uses weight 0.3 and recursively accumulates Poisson rate variance, starting at baseline with zero variance. Both are one-sided. Baseline selection should be justified and stability checked by a statistician; zero baselines are rejected. This simple model assumes independent counts and comparable exposure, and does not correct for multiple comparisons, delayed complaints or overdispersion. A signal starts investigation; it is not itself an Article 88 determination.

The JSON record preserves normalized events, rule citations, hazard references, exposure hashes and a normalized-record hash. It is a draft snapshot, not immutable storage, an electronic signature, or a controlled approval workflow. Retain and approve records in an appropriate quality system. Hazard mapping alone does not estimate risk or demonstrate ISO 14971 compliance.
